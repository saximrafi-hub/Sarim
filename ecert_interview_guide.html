opening script
"Hi Manzar, thanks for making the time. Quick context before we dive in: this is a pure account management role. Keeping existing customers happy, managing renewals, and growing within those accounts. Not about hunting new business or cold calling. You'll be the primary contact for a portfolio of care sector clients. Does that make sense?"
CV clarification
~8 mins▼
Note: DOB 1-Aug-2005. Claims 5+ years but he's 20. Employment started 2021 when he was 15. Probe carefully.
Q1
You've had quite a few roles over the past few years. Walk me through each move — why did you leave?
Probe: What were you looking for? Was there a pattern?
Red: vague / just moneyGreen: clear learning progression
Q2
At TradeWheel.com you describe converting prospects into "sustainable, long-term partnerships." Once you closed a deal, did you stay involved with the client?
Probe: How often did you speak to them post-close? Did you manage their satisfaction?
Red: handed off after saleGreen: stayed involved, regular check-ins
Q3
At Beekay Outsourcing you led a team of 8. Were they managing accounts or hunting new business? What were their KPIs?
Probe: What was your biggest challenge as a leader?
Red: can't recall specificsGreen: clear team structure and targets
Q4
You've worked across US, Caribbean, Africa, Europe, and MENA. What made those markets different? How did you adapt?
Probe: Communication style, expectations, pace?
Tests cultural adaptability for UK clients
Account management understanding
~7 mins▼
Q5
Have you ever been responsible for keeping a customer happy after the initial sale? What did that look like?
Probe: How often did you check in? Did you track renewal dates or contract timelines?
Red: no post-sale responsibilityGreen: regular touchpoints, tracked contracts
Q6
This role is retention and renewals, not new business. How do you feel about that shift from your sales background?
Probe: What specifically appeals to you about AM over continuing in sales?
Red: reluctant, just wants commissionGreen: sees relationship depth as the goal
Scenarios
~10 mins▼
scenario 1
"You've just inherited 12 care home clients. Your first task is a quarterly account review call. How do you prepare and what do you cover?"
Probe: What information do you want before the call?
Bad: no structure, wings itGood: reviews data, prepares customer questions, documents
scenario 2
"A customer tells you mid-contract: 'We're not getting enough value.' It's 6 months before renewal. What do you do?"
Probe: What if they say they want to cancel?
Bad: defensive, gives upGood: listens, escalates, proposes solution
scenario 3
"A customer has gone quiet for 2 months — no emails opened, no platform logins, no replies. What's your move?"
Probe: How would you even know they were quiet in the first place?
Bad: wouldn't notice, no monitoringGood: proactive tracking, switches to phone/WhatsApp, treats urgently
scenario 4
"A customer wants to renew but downgrade to a cheaper plan. They say they've cut costs. How do you handle it?"
Probe: Would you give them a discount if they asked?
Bad: inflexible or gives away margin aloneGood: explores options, escalates for approval
CRM, channels & logistics
~8 mins▼
Q7
At TradeWheel you used calls, chats, and Zoom. Did you log these in a CRM? Which one — and how deep was your usage?
Note: We use HubSpot. Any CRM is acceptable; assess willingness to learn.
Red: no CRM experience and resistant to learningGreen: any CRM, logged interactions, tracked data
Q8
You'll manage customers via email, WhatsApp, phone calls, and live chat. Comfortable across all four?
Probe: Which is your strongest? Any you'd be less comfortable with?
Red: avoids phoneGreen: confident across all channels
Q9 — quick scenario
A customer sends an angry WhatsApp about a billing issue at 6 PM. What's your immediate response?
Bad: ignores until next dayGood: acknowledges, escalates, commits to timeline
